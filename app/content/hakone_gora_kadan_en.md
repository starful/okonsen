## Introduction

In the lexicon of Japanese luxury ryokan, certain names occupy a place entirely their own. **Gora Kadan** (強羅花壇) is one of them. Set on the terraced gardens of a former Imperial summer villa in the Gora district of Hakone, this Relais & Châteaux member property combines historical gravitas, meticulous natural beauty, and a standard of hospitality that has earned it recognition as one of Japan's finest ryokans.

The approach sets the tone immediately. Beyond the gate, a series of stone-walled garden terraces unfolds — the same terraces designed for Prince Takamatsu (弟宮裕仁親王), younger brother of Emperor Hirohito, whose summer villa occupied this site in the 1940s. Carefully maintained seasonal plantings, mature trees, and the clean lines of traditional garden design create an environment that breathes with a kind of curated serenity unavailable in hotels that haven't had decades of dedicated care. On a clear day, guests looking southwest from the upper gardens can sometimes catch a glimpse of Mount Fuji above the Hakone ridgeline — a view that was presumably one of the reasons the imperial family chose this particular hillside.

Gora Kadan is not the most accessible introduction to Japanese ryokan culture — its pricing reflects its pedigree and positioning, and the experience is designed for guests who know what they are seeking. But for those who have experienced multiple ryokans and want to understand what the format can achieve at its apex, or for those marking a genuinely significant occasion with a once-in-a-lifetime stay, it represents something quite apart from the ordinary.

## Imperial History

The land on which Gora Kadan stands was selected as the site of a summer retreat for Prince Takamatsu, one of the younger brothers of Emperor Hirohito. The property was developed in the early Showa period and functioned as a private imperial vacation residence, its gardens laid out in the terraced style that takes advantage of the natural slope of the Gora hillside above the valley. The terraced design — unusual in traditional Japanese garden aesthetics, which typically favor more organically shaped land forms — was adapted from the unique topography of the site, creating a series of distinct garden rooms that descend the hillside and frame views across the Hakone caldera.

Following the postwar reorganization of imperial household properties, the estate was eventually converted into a ryokan, opening to private guests and building a reputation across subsequent decades that culminated in its recognition by Relais & Châteaux — the French association of independent luxury hotels and restaurants that counts only properties meeting exceptionally high standards in architecture, cuisine, and service. Gora Kadan's membership in Relais & Châteaux is not decorative; it reflects a sustained commitment to operational excellence across every aspect of the guest experience.

The history infuses the stay with a specific quality of place-weight that newly built properties cannot manufacture: these terraces were designed for an imperial household, maintained through war and postwar transition, and now open to anyone willing to invest in the experience. That layering of past into present is one of the intangible pleasures of Gora Kadan.

## Baths & Spring Water

The onsen experience at Gora Kadan is distinguished by its **emphasis on privacy**. Each guest room is equipped with its own private outdoor bath (*rotenburo*), ensuring that the signature pleasure of Hakone's geothermal springs is available at any hour without competition or self-consciousness. This approach — the private rotenburo as a standard feature rather than a premium upgrade — fundamentally shapes the rhythm of a stay: guests bathe when the mood calls for it, whether at midnight, at dawn, or between courses of a long afternoon.

The spring water at Gora Kadan is drawn from Hakone's geothermal sources and varies by batch, as is common in the area. The Hakone volcanic region produces waters of varied compositions, and the ryokan takes care to maintain the quality and temperature of the baths throughout the year. The water is typically clear to slightly opaque, with a mild mineral quality and the characteristic softness that Hakone bathers have prized for generations.

Beyond the in-room private baths, the property maintains **shared indoor and outdoor baths** for guests who wish to use communal facilities — though the in-room experience is the defining feature and primary draw.

**Tattoo policy**: As a high-end traditional ryokan, Gora Kadan's policies on tattoos in communal baths should be confirmed directly at time of booking. Guests with tattoos are typically best served by the private in-room rotenburo, which are available to all guests regardless.

## Rooms & Accommodation

The guest rooms at Gora Kadan are arranged across several buildings on the terraced estate, including both the main wing and separate villa-style pavilions. Accommodations are primarily in the **traditional Japanese style** (*washitsu*) — tatami floors, *shoji* screen windows, and futon bedding prepared by staff in the evening — though some rooms incorporate contemporary touches and *wafu-yoshitsu* (Japanese-Western hybrid) elements with both futon and bed options.

All rooms include private onsen baths (rotenburo or *hinoki* cedar baths), and the better rooms are positioned to take advantage of the estate's garden and valley views. Premium rooms and suites occupy the most favored positions on the terraces, with wider garden aspects and more expansive outdoor spaces.

The furnishing and object selection throughout the property reflects a Japanese aesthetic of restraint and precision: high-quality textiles, lacquerware, seasonal flower arrangements (*ikebana*), and ceramics chosen with the quiet authority of long practice. Nothing is superfluous. The overall atmosphere is one of composed elegance — thoughtful rather than showy, deeply Japanese rather than internationally generic.

Standard in-room inclusions span high-quality *yukata*, bath towels and amenities, in-room tea service with premium green teas, and comprehensive toiletries. The level of individual attention from staff — the *omotenashi* that defines the best ryokan service — is consistently noted by guests as among the most refined they have encountered in Japan.

## Dining

The kaiseki cuisine at Gora Kadan is recognized as one of the finest in the Hakone region and is a significant part of the ryokan's reputation within the Relais & Châteaux network. The kitchen operates with the same principles that govern the best kaiseki restaurants: total seasonal alignment, ingredients sourced at their peak, and execution that balances classical form with individual expression.

A typical dinner progression at Gora Kadan follows the classic kaiseki arc — from delicate aperitif-style *sakizuke* through *hassun* (the seasonal platter), clear *owan* soup, *mukōzuke* (raw seafood course), grilled *yakimono*, a simmered *takiawase*, a fried or steamed dish, and finally the rice course with pickles and *miso* soup, concluded by seasonal sweets and matcha. Each dish is presented in ceramics selected to complement both the food and the season, and the progression is calibrated to build a complete sensory narrative across the meal's two or more hours.

The seafood comes from the waters of Sagami Bay and beyond; the mountain vegetables and wild herbs from the Hakone uplands; the wagyu from carefully selected producers in Japan's premium beef regions. The rice course — the moment in kaiseki when the meal pauses and the kitchen's most fundamental craft is revealed — is invariably the quality of Kanagawa's best.

Breakfast is an equally serious affair, served in-room and presenting a broad array of carefully prepared dishes from hot grilled fish to cold *tofu* preparations, pickles, and clear soup. The pacing is generous; no one is hurried.

Dining rooms are available for guests who prefer a more formal setting outside their rooms, and the experience there is equally refined.

## Garden & Grounds

The terraced garden is as much a feature of Gora Kadan as the onsen or the cuisine, and time spent simply moving through it — or sitting in one of the quiet garden spaces watching the seasons play out — is not wasted time. The gardens are maintained with the intensive attention that traditional Japanese garden care demands: each tree shaped, each stone considered, each seasonal planting timed to succession so that something is always at its moment of transient perfection.

In **spring**, Japanese cherries and plum trees bloom across the terraces. In **summer**, the deep greens of maple and cedar intensify against the mountain backdrop. In **autumn**, the garden transforms into a composition of crimson and gold that rivals anything the landscape designers could have planned. In **winter**, when Hakone occasionally receives light snow, the terraces take on an austere beauty that residents of warmer climates find extraordinary.

The garden's terraced structure, a legacy of its imperial origins, creates a sequence of distinct spaces rather than a single panorama — an intimate series of garden rooms that guests encounter one by one as they move through the estate.

## Local Area

Gora Kadan's location in the **Gora district** of Hakone places it within reach of the full spectrum of Hakone's attractions. The Hakone Tozan Cablecar terminus is nearby, linking Gora to the ropeway and the crater area at Owakudani, where the famous volcanic sulfur vents are visible and the black tamago (eggs boiled in the sulfurous pools) can be eaten for luck.

**Hakone Open-Air Museum** (*Chokoku no Mori*), one of Japan's finest sculpture parks, is accessible by the Hakone Tozan Railway and presents an extraordinary collection of modern and contemporary sculpture in an outdoor setting. The combination of art, mountain views, and a day that ends at Gora Kadan's rotenburo is close to ideal.

**Lake Ashi** (*Ashinoko*) — where on a clear day the famous view of Mount Fuji reflected in the lake's surface opens up — is reachable by ropeway from Owakudani or by the Hakone-en bus. **Hakone Venetian Glass Museum**, **Pola Museum of Art**, and multiple traditional craft galleries and tea houses fill out a cultural itinerary that never feels repetitive in the Hakone area.

## Practical Tips

**Reservations**: Gora Kadan is consistently in high demand, particularly for premium rooms and during spring (cherry blossom season) and autumn (foliage). Bookings several months in advance are standard for peak periods. Reservations can be made via the official website, through Relais & Châteaux's booking platform, or through selected travel agents with access to premium Japanese ryokan inventory.

**Check-in and check-out**: Check-in is typically from 3:00 PM; check-out by 11:00 AM or noon. Guests are encouraged to arrive at the beginning of check-in time to maximize evening bath and dining time.

**Dress**: In-house movement in *yukata* and slippers is customary. Dinner may be attended in *yukata* or in quiet casual dress; formal Western attire is neither required nor expected.

**Language**: Gora Kadan maintains a multilingual staff capable of assisting international guests. English-language service is reliably available.

**Mount Fuji visibility**: Clear views of Fuji from the upper terraces and from the Gora area generally are more likely in winter and spring, when humidity is lower. Summer and early autumn often bring haze that obscures the mountain. This is worth bearing in mind when selecting a visit date if the view is important.

## Access

**By Hakone Tozan Railway**:
From Odawara Station (accessible from Tokyo via the Odakyu Romance Car, the Tokaido Shinkansen, or the JR Tokaido Line), take the Hakone Tozan Railway bound for Gora. The journey from Odawara to Gora Station takes approximately 35–40 minutes. From Gora Station, Gora Kadan is approximately a 7–10 minute walk up the hill, or a short taxi ride.

**Romancecar from Shinjuku**:
The Odakyu Romancecar Limited Express from Shinjuku Station runs directly to Hakone-Yumoto Station (approximately 85 minutes), from which the Hakone Tozan Railway connects onward to Gora. The total journey from Shinjuku to Gora takes approximately 2 hours. The Romancecar offers advance-reserved seating and is comfortable; booking is recommended particularly on weekends.

**By Shinkansen**:
The Tokaido Shinkansen (Kodama or Hikari) from Tokyo Station reaches Odawara in approximately 35 minutes. From Odawara, onward connections to Gora by train take an additional 40 minutes.

**By Car**:
From Tokyo, take the Tomei Expressway to the Odawara-Atsugi Road or the Hakone Turnpike and proceed toward the Gora area. Drive time is approximately 1.5 to 2 hours depending on traffic. Parking is available at the ryokan; the approach road is narrow in places.

**Ryokan transfer**: Gora Kadan offers transport arrangements from Gora Station and can assist guests in coordinating their arrival. Confirm any transfer options at the time of booking.

The approach to Gora Kadan from the station climbs gently uphill through the quiet residential district of Gora — past the sounds of the Hakone Tozan trains, the smell of cedar, and the increasing sense that the ordinary world has, once again, been left at a sufficient distance.
