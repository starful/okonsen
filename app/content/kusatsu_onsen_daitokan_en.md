## Introduction

Deep in the mountains of Gunma Prefecture, at the heart of the Japanese archipelago, lies Kusatsu Onsen — known as the "waters of healing," one of Japan's three great onsen with a thousand-year history. In the center of this vibrant hot spring town, within reach of the steam rising from the famous Yubatake, sits "Kusatsu Onsen Daitokan," quiet and unassuming. Daitokan goes beyond simply providing accommodation; it is a ryokan designed to let guests fully experience the intense allure and local character of Kusatsu Onsen.

The moment you step across the threshold, you can feel Japan's distinctive, meticulous hospitality — *omotenashi* — in a space where tradition and modernity meet in harmony. Outside the window, the lively scene of steam billowing up from the Yubatake unfolds, and beneath it, the energy radiating from Kusatsu's powerful spring water seeps even into the guest rooms. Daitokan is more than just a place to rest — it is the ideal sanctuary for those who wish to deeply understand and love the essence of Kusatsu Onsen, and a special space that creates unforgettable memories. Here, purify body and mind, and lose yourself in the ecstasy of experiencing the very essence of Japan's onsen culture.

## History & Tradition

Kusatsu Onsen boasts a deep history spanning more than 1,200 years, its healing properties having been widely known since ancient times. Its renown runs so deep that records tell of famous Japanese generals and warriors visiting to heal their wounds and relieve their fatigue. Daitokan carries forward this long history and tradition of Kusatsu Onsen, while adding modern comforts along the way.

Throughout the ryokan, one can sense the effort made to respect and preserve the culture unique to Kusatsu Onsen. In particular, "*yumomi*," the symbolic ritual of Kusatsu Onsen — the traditional method of stirring the water with wooden boards to lower its temperature — is performed regularly at the Yubatake near Daitokan, letting visitors vividly feel the region's lively tradition. Daitokan is deeply connected to this local tradition, offering guests the opportunity to experience not merely soaking in the bath, but the history and culture of Kusatsu Onsen itself. The refined atmosphere within the ryokan, its traditional architectural style, and its meticulous service shine all the more brightly within this historical context, offering visitors the special thrill of stepping back in time.

## Baths & Water

The greatest characteristic and pride of Kusatsu Onsen is, without question, its overwhelming water quality. Recognized as one of Japan's most strongly acidic sulfur springs, it boasts abundant sulfur content and powerful sterilizing effects, earning the reputation as "the onsen that heals every illness." Daitokan channels this legendary spring water directly from the heart of Kusatsu, allowing guests to fully enjoy the true effects of the onsen.

Daitokan offers a variety of onsen facilities.

### Open-Air Bath (Rotenburo)

Nestled as if buried in nature, the open-air bath is the space where the atmosphere of Kusatsu Onsen can be felt most vividly. Soaking in the steaming spring water while taking in the scenery that changes with each season, the bustle of the city naturally melts away, replaced by deep tranquility. Bathing amid a snowy landscape in particular offers an experience you will never forget. The strongly acidic water may feel somewhat stimulating on the skin at first, but it soon relieves fatigue throughout the body and leaves a sensation of smoother, softer skin.

### Large Communal Bath (Daiyokujo)

The spacious and comfortable indoor communal bath exudes a cozy yet traditional Japanese onsen atmosphere. The subtle scent of wood combined with the warm water enveloping the whole body is excellent for easing tension in body and mind. Separated for men and women, it can be enjoyed comfortably, with ample changing space and washing facilities well equipped.

### Paid Private Bath (Kashikiri-buro) or Family Bath

Some ryokan offer a paid, reservation-based private bath or family bath. When visiting Daitokan, it is worth confirming in advance whether such a private space is available and how to use it. For those who wish to enjoy the onsen intimately with a partner or family in a private setting, this is an excellent option.

Kusatsu's spring water is known to be particularly effective for chronic skin conditions, muscle pain, neuralgia, and fatigue recovery. Daitokan's onsen facilities are meticulously managed to let guests experience Kusatsu's exceptional water quality in an optimal setting.

**Tattoo Policy:** Guests with tattoos may face restrictions when using shared facilities such as the large communal bath. We recommend contacting the ryokan directly before your visit to confirm the current policy. Typically, small tattoos may be covered with a patch, or guests are often encouraged to use the private bath instead.

## Rooms & Architecture

The guest rooms at Kusatsu Onsen Daitokan reinterpret the aesthetics of traditional Japanese architecture with a modern sensibility, offering both comfort and atmosphere at once. Most rooms are Japanese-style *washitsu* with neat tatami flooring, and the subtle fragrance of tatami has a calming effect on the mind.

### Japanese-Style Room (Washitsu)

A traditional Japanese tatami room where, upon opening the shoji sliding doors, the outside scenery unfolds like a painting. During the day, enjoy tea and conversation in the spacious room; at night, cozy futon bedding is laid out to provide a comfortable place to sleep. The minimal yet delicate design embodies the Japanese spirit of *wabi-sabi*, emphasizing harmony with nature. Within the room, yukata, towels, and basic toiletries are provided, along with Kusatsu's famous onsen manju (steamed buns) and green tea — allowing your ryokan experience to begin the moment you enter your room.

### Japanese-Western Style Room (Wayoushitsu)

Some rooms are configured as *wayoushitsu*, combining a traditional tatami space with a Western-style area featuring a bed. This is an ideal choice for those who wish to feel the atmosphere of tatami while preferring the comfort of a bed. Through large windows, you can spend a leisurely time taking in the view of the Kusatsu Onsen town or the surrounding green nature.

### Special Rooms and View Rooms

Daitokan also offers view rooms that capture the dynamic scenery of the Yubatake at a glance, as well as special rooms equipped with larger, more luxurious facilities. From these rooms, you can watch the steam rising from the Yubatake at night, feeling even more deeply the unique atmosphere of Kusatsu Onsen. The breathtaking view unfolding beyond the window makes the room itself feel like a work of art.

The ryokan's overall architecture predominantly uses natural materials such as wood and natural stone, pursuing harmony with the surrounding environment. Common spaces like the lobby and corridors also preserve the beauty of traditional Japanese style while not overlooking modern convenience, allowing visitors to spend their time in comfort and ease.

## Dining (Kaiseki)

Dining at Daitokan goes beyond simply satisfying hunger — it is akin to a work of art that satisfies all five senses. In particular, dinner is served as *kaiseki*, the traditional Japanese course meal, standing as one of the highlights of any ryokan stay.

### Dinner: Kaiseki Cuisine

Daitokan's kaiseki cuisine is characterized by making the most of the freshness of seasonal ingredients while adding the distinctive flavors unique to Gunma Prefecture. Delicate dishes that delight both eyes and palate are composed as follows.

*   **Sakizuke:** A small appetizer heralding the arrival of the season, notable for its delicate flavor and beautiful presentation.
*   **Zensai:** A variety of appetizers made with diverse ingredients and cooking methods, serving to stimulate the appetite.
*   **Owan:** A clear soup with a deep, clean flavor, warming the body after the onsen bath.
*   **Sashimi:** Made with fresh, seasonal fish, showcasing the essence of Japanese cuisine.
*   **Yakimono:** Grilled dishes may feature Gunma specialties such as the succulent Joshu beef. Its tender texture and rich flavor add to the culinary pleasure.
*   **Nimono:** A refined simmered dish made with seasonal vegetables or seafood.
*   **Agemono:** Crisp, savory tempura offers yet another delight.
*   **Shokuji:** Consisting of freshly cooked rice, miso soup, and pickled side dishes (*konomono*).
*   **Mizugashi:** A clean finish featuring seasonal fruit or traditional Japanese sweets.

Each dish is crafted from carefully selected ingredients and the skill of experienced chefs to bring out its finest flavor, and is presented on beautiful tableware for visual delight as well. Pairing your meal with local sake or beverages recommended by the ryokan makes for an even richer culinary experience.

### Breakfast

Breakfast is generally served in the traditional Japanese style. A nutritionally balanced, healthy, and refined spread — including freshly cooked rice, miso soup, grilled fish, egg dishes, and pickles — provides more than enough energy to start the day.

Meals are typically served in the ryokan's restaurant or an individual dining room, and some rooms allow guests to enjoy meals in the comfort of their own space. It is a good idea to confirm the dining location and time upon your visit.

## Local Area

While staying at Kusatsu Onsen Daitokan, exploring the vibrant town of Kusatsu Onsen beyond simple relaxation within the ryokan is an equally unmissable pleasure. Daitokan is centrally located in the town, offering excellent access to major attractions.

### Yubatake

The Yubatake, the symbol and heart of Kusatsu Onsen, is within very easy walking distance of Daitokan. With steam billowing forth and the scent of sulfur permeating the air, the place resembles a giant open-air laboratory. Hot spring water flows along wooden channels, naturally cooling as it goes, creating a unique landscape as sulfur compounds crystallize in the process. At night, lights illuminate the area around the Yubatake, creating a fantastical night view — well worth a visit and a stroll. A foot bath is also set up near the Yubatake, allowing you to casually enjoy the onsen experience.

### Sainokawara Park

A roughly 10-minute walk from the Yubatake brings you to Sainokawara Park, a natural, untouched open-air onsen area. Hot spring water rises up throughout the park, forming small ponds, and the mystical scene of rising steam offers a captivating sight. At the far end of the park lies the expansive open-air bath, Sainokawara Rotenburo, allowing you to fully enjoy the onsen amid grand nature. The area is also lovely for a walk amid beautiful natural scenery in every season.

### Onsen Manju and Souvenir Shopping

The streets of Kusatsu Onsen town are lined with numerous onsen manju shops and souvenir stores. You can savor freshly steamed, warm onsen manju, or browse charming souvenirs such as soap, bath salts, and pottery crafted to showcase the characteristics of Kusatsu Onsen.

### Yumomi Performance and Dance Show

At Netsu-no-Yu, located next to the Yubatake, you can watch a performance of *yumomi*, the traditional Kusatsu Onsen ritual. This traditional ceremony of stirring the hot spring water with wooden boards to cool it is accompanied by lively songs and dance, offering a distinctive spectacle. It is best to check the performance schedule in advance.

### Walking and Hiking Nearby

The area surrounding Kusatsu Onsen is enveloped by beautiful mountains and forests, offering everything from light strolls to full-fledged hiking. Autumn foliage creates a spectacular scene, while winter offers a fantastical snowy landscape.

Using Daitokan as your base, we hope you fully enjoy the vibrancy of Kusatsu Onsen town alongside the beauty of nature, creating a rich variety of experiences.

## Practical Tips

Here is some practical information to make your stay at Kusatsu Onsen Daitokan even smoother and more enjoyable.

### Reservations and Check-In/Check-Out

*   **Reservations:** Rooms can fill up quickly, especially on weekends, Japanese holidays, and during peak onsen seasons (autumn foliage, winter snow season, etc.), so we recommend booking at least a few weeks in advance.
*   **Check-In/Check-Out Times:** Check-in is generally at 3:00 PM, and check-out around 10:00 or 11:00 AM. It is best to confirm the exact times at the time of booking or by contacting the ryokan directly.

### Ryokan Etiquette

*   **Using the Onsen:** It is proper etiquette to thoroughly shower and wash your body before entering shared onsen facilities like the large communal bath. Do not bring your towel into the bath itself, and long hair should be tied up so it does not touch the water. Please enjoy the onsen quietly and calmly.
*   **Yukata:** The ryokan provides a yukata. You may wear it comfortably throughout the ryokan and move freely between the dining area, onsen, and all shared spaces. Please observe proper yukata etiquette (the left side of the collar should be on top).
*   **Meal Times:** Since kaiseki cuisine is served at a set time, it is best to be punctual for meals.

### Language

*   Given the nature of Japanese ryokan, English communication can sometimes be somewhat limited. Preparing simple Japanese phrases or a translation app will make things more convenient. Daitokan's staff may be able to communicate basic English, though in-depth conversation may be difficult.

### What to Bring

*   The ryokan provides yukata, slippers, towels, and basic toiletries (shampoo, conditioner, body wash). It is a good idea to bring any personal cosmetics or specific toiletries you use.
*   It is also good to bring light clothing or pajamas that are comfortable to wear after bathing.

### A Note on Tattoo Policy

*   As mentioned earlier, Japanese ryokan have varying policies regarding tattoos. It is best to reconfirm Daitokan's tattoo policy in advance to ensure there are no issues using the shared onsen. Generally, small tattoos are often permitted if covered with a patch.

### On-Site Amenities

*   Most ryokan feature a souvenir shop, lounge, and vending machines.
*   Check whether free Wi-Fi is available and usable within the guest room.

We hope this practical information helps make your stay at Daitokan an even more comfortable and enjoyable experience.

## Access

Kusatsu Onsen Daitokan is located deep within Gunma Prefecture, but is relatively easy to access from major cities including Tokyo. When using public transportation, the main options are combining train and bus, or taking a direct express bus.

### 1. By Train and Bus from Tokyo

This method is recommended for those who want to travel at a leisurely pace while enjoying Japan's beautiful scenery from the train.

*   **By Shinkansen:**
    *   From Tokyo Station or Ueno Station, take the JR Joetsu Shinkansen or JR Hokuriku Shinkansen to Takasaki Station or Karuizawa Station.
    *   **Example (via Joetsu Shinkansen):** Tokyo Station — (approx. 50 minutes to 1 hour) — Takasaki Station
*   **By Local/Rapid Line:**
    *   From Takasaki Station or Karuizawa Station, transfer to the JR Agatsuma Line to Naganoharakusatsuguchi Station.
    *   **Example (from Takasaki Station):** Takasaki Station — (approx. 1 hour 20 minutes) — Naganoharakusatsuguchi Station
*   **By Local Bus:**
    *   Upon arriving at Naganoharakusatsuguchi Station, transfer to the JR bus bound for the Kusatsu Onsen Bus Terminal directly in front of the station. The bus ride takes about 25 to 30 minutes.
*   **From Kusatsu Onsen Bus Terminal to Daitokan:**
    *   From the Kusatsu Onsen Bus Terminal, it is about a 5- to 10-minute walk to Daitokan. If you have a lot of luggage or find walking difficult, a taxi is also available in front of the terminal.

The total travel time, including transfer waiting times, is expected to be around 3 to 4 hours.

### 2. By Direct Express Bus from Tokyo

This is the most convenient and straightforward method, well-suited for those who want to travel from central Tokyo to Kusatsu Onsen in one trip.

*   **Departure Point:** From Shinjuku Bus Terminal (Busta Shinjuku) in Tokyo, take a bus bound for Kusatsu Onsen operated by JR Kanto Bus or other express bus companies.
*   **Destination:** Kusatsu Onsen Bus Terminal.
*   **Travel Time:** Approximately 4 hours to 4 hours 30 minutes.
*   **From the Bus Terminal to Daitokan:** Travel on foot or by taxi upon arrival.

The express bus has the advantage of being direct, with no transfer hassle, and is relatively affordable.

### By Car

*   From Tokyo, take the Kan-etsu Expressway, exit at Shibukawa-Ikaho IC or Usui-Karuizawa IC, and follow the national highway toward Kusatsu Onsen.
*   The ryokan has parking facilities, but during peak season, parking spaces may be limited, so advance confirmation is recommended.

Whichever mode of transportation you choose, the journey to Kusatsu Onsen will be one where you can fully enjoy Japan's beautiful scenery. Choose the method best suited to your travel style, and begin your comfortable journey to Daitokan.
