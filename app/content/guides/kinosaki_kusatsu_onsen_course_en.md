If you want the postcard version of Japanese onsen towns, **Kinosaki** and **Kusatsu** are the cleanest pairing: one is about strolling in *yukata* between public baths, the other about serious mineral water and mountain air.

## How to sequence them

They are not next-door neighbors. Treat this as **two separate legs** on a longer Japan trip (Kansai ↔ Gunma/Tokyo axis), not a casual same-day hop.

**Suggested:** 2 nights Kinosaki → travel day → 1–2 nights Kusatsu → Tokyo or reverse.

## Kinosaki — bath passport nights

Check into a ryokan, change into yukata, and hop the town’s public baths with a *yu-meguri* passport. Dinner at the ryokan still matters — the street hop is the evening theater.

## Kusatsu — strong water, highland calm

Kusatsu’s *yumomi* show and outdoor yubatake are worth seeing once, but the stay is about the baths. Sulfuric water can be intense — start with shorter soaks.

## Practical tips

*   Book weekends early in both towns
*   Bring a small towel for public baths; follow local tattoo rules
*   For Kusatsu, confirm bus/train connections from Karuizawa or Naganohara-Kusatsuguchi
