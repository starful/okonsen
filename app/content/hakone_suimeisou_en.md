## Introduction

Hakone, one of Japan's most iconic hot spring destinations, has long drawn travelers with its majestic mountains, crystal-clear lakes, and abundant thermal springs. Amid this splendor lies a special sanctuary for those seeking true rest and refined experiences: Hakone Suimeisou. More than a simple ryokan, it is celebrated as a destination that offers complete rejuvenation amid the grandeur of nature. Its crowning feature is the private family bath, where guests can savor Hakone's serene scenery in complete solitude — an especially precious moment for families and couples alike. At Hakone Suimeisou, where meticulous hospitality and traditional aesthetics blend harmoniously with modern comfort, we invite you to step away from the bustle of daily life and discover genuine peace.

## History & Tradition

Hakone Suimeisou builds upon the rich historical backdrop of Hakone, a hot spring region with a long and storied past, adding a contemporary sensibility while carrying forward the essence of the traditional Japanese ryokan. The hospitality know-how accumulated over years of welcoming guests manifests in *omotenashi*, Japan's distinctive culture of wholehearted service. From the moment guests step through the door until the moment they depart, every staff member places the comfort and satisfaction of each individual guest above all else, offering meticulous care without compromise. The building's architectural style and interior design also offer glimpses of traditional Japanese architectural beauty. Structures that respect harmony with nature, featuring low eaves and timber that do not disrupt the surrounding landscape, create an atmosphere both serene and elegant. Modern amenities are subtly woven into this tradition, allowing visitors to feel the beauty of the old while enjoying the utmost in comfort. Hakone Suimeisou is not merely an accommodation facility — it is akin to a living work of art, still breathing within the flow of Japanese culture and history.

## Baths & Water

The onsen at Hakone Suimeisou is truly the heart and soul of this ryokan. The mineral-rich spring water, rising from deep beneath Hakone, is known for being gentle on the skin and remarkably effective at relieving bodily fatigue. Typically containing sulfur and chloride components, it is said to improve blood circulation, ease neuralgia and muscle pain, and leave the skin beautifully smooth.

### Private Family Baths: The Ultimate Retreat Amid Great Views

The greatest allure of Hakone Suimeisou is undoubtedly its "private family baths." Designed against a backdrop of Hakone's sweeping natural scenery, these baths ensure that only you and your companion can enjoy the onsen in quiet, cozy privacy. Most of the family baths take the form of open-air rotenburo, allowing guests to savor Hakone's ever-shifting seasonal landscapes with all five senses while bathing. The scenery — cherry blossoms in spring, verdant green in summer, vivid foliage in autumn, and snow-covered vistas in winter — is a living painting in itself. Surrounded by tall walls that guarantee complete privacy, guests can experience true relaxation in a spacious setting, listening intently to the sounds of nature. Family baths generally operate on a reservation basis for specific time slots; whether they are included in the room rate or incur a separate usage fee may vary, so it is best to confirm at the time of booking.

### The Large Communal Bath: A Space for Contemplation

Beyond the private family baths, the ryokan also features separate large communal baths for men and women. These, too, offer both outdoor and indoor baths with views of Hakone's nature, allowing guests to enjoy the onsen in a more spacious setting. Impeccably maintained, the large communal bath not only soothes bodily fatigue but is also well suited to quiet contemplation amid its tranquil atmosphere. Guests can take time to soak in the spring water, breathe in the rising steam, and wash away the cares of everyday life.

### Tattoo Policy

As with many onsen facilities in Japan, Hakone Suimeisou may have policies regarding visible tattoos. Generally, Japanese ryokan often restrict entry to their large communal baths for guests with tattoos, or ask that even small tattoos be covered. However, the privately reservable family baths and in-room open-air baths are, for the most part, free of such restrictions. It is wise to contact the ryokan directly before your visit to confirm their exact tattoo policy.

## Rooms & Architecture

The guest rooms at Hakone Suimeisou are spaces that perfectly combine the aesthetics of traditional Japanese architecture with modern comfort. Every room is designed to harmonize with the majestic nature of Hakone, giving the sense that the beautiful scenery outside the window flows seamlessly into the room itself.

### The Beauty of Tradition and the Comfort of Modernity

Rooms are broadly divided into the traditional Japanese-style *washitsu* and the hybrid *wayoushitsu*, which blends Japanese and Western styles. The washitsu, with its tatami flooring and *shoji* sliding doors, allows guests to fully feel the distinctly serene and tranquil atmosphere unique to Japan. The soft texture underfoot on the tatami and its subtle fragrance make one forget the clamor of the city. The wayoushitsu is furnished with Western-style beds for added comfort, while still preserving a tatami area to retain the character of a Japanese ryokan. Modern furnishings and traditional decorative pieces are arranged with great care, showcasing a sophisticated beauty.

### In-Room Open-Air Baths: An Utterly Personal Retreat

Notably, some of the premium rooms come with their own private open-air bath, allowing guests to fully enjoy Hakone's nature and an onsen soak in an entirely personal space. Being able to immerse yourself in warm spring water whenever you wish, free from the gaze of others, is the ultimate luxury. In-room open-air baths are typically located on an outdoor balcony, designed to offer a panoramic view of the surrounding forest, valley, or mountain range. An onsen enjoyed amid the misty scenery of dawn, a sky dyed by the evening sunset, or beneath a star-filled night sky will leave you with unforgettable memories.

### A Space of Thoughtful Consideration

Every room is furnished with premium bedding and amenities, along with a space for enjoying tea, a mini bar, and the latest facilities. Outside the window, a view unfolds that lets you fully feel the changing seasons of Hakone. The ryokan's architecture is designed to make the most of the natural terrain while harmonizing with the surrounding environment, using nature-friendly materials such as wood, stone, and earth to create a warm and comfortable atmosphere. Each room maintains its own independence while sharing the ryokan's unified aesthetic overall, offering guests genuine tranquility and comfort.

## Dining (Kaiseki)

Dining at Hakone Suimeisou goes far beyond a simple meal — it is the pinnacle of a culinary experience distilled from traditional Japanese art and aesthetics. In particular, the *kaiseki* cuisine served at dinner is one of the highlights of any ryokan stay.

### Kaiseki Capturing the Essence of the Four Seasons

Kaiseki cuisine is a traditional Japanese course meal that draws out the flavor and aroma of seasonal ingredients through delicate preparation, presented with plating that delights the eyes as much as the palate. The chefs at Hakone Suimeisou create their dishes using the finest ingredients — fresh seafood caught in the nearby Sagami Bay, mountain vegetables and produce native to Hakone, and carefully selected premium wagyu beef. As the menu shifts fluidly with the changing seasons, guests can look forward to discovering new flavors with every visit.

The course begins with a *sakizuke* appetizer, followed in turn by *sashimi* featuring fresh seasonal fish, a warm clear soup (*owan*), grilled dishes (*yakimono*), simmered dishes (*nimono*), fried dishes (*agemono*), and steamed dishes (*mushimono*), prepared through a wide variety of cooking methods. Each dish arrives neatly arranged on a small plate, its composition carefully considered for balance of color, texture, and flavor. The tableware itself is also an important element that further highlights the beauty of the food, with pieces crafted by master artisans, from ceramics to lacquerware.

### An Experience That Satisfies All Five Senses

Kaiseki cuisine is an art form that satisfies not only taste but sight and smell as well — all five senses. Every single dish carries the chef's craftsmanship and reverence for the nature of Hakone. The local sake or wine paired with the meal further enriches the culinary experience.

### An Elegant Japanese Breakfast

Breakfast is generally served in the traditional Japanese *washoku* style. Consisting of freshly cooked rice, miso soup, grilled fish, and an array of Japanese side dishes, it allows guests to begin their day feeling both satisfied and healthy. Breakfast, too, showcases fresh ingredients and refined presentation, offering a satisfaction on par with the evening's kaiseki.

### The Dining Environment

Meals can be enjoyed comfortably within your room, or served in one of the ryokan's individual dining rooms. Guests can quietly focus on their meal in a private space, spending precious time with loved ones. If you have food allergies or specific dietary restrictions, informing the ryokan in advance at the time of booking will allow them to accommodate your needs as much as possible.

## Local Area

Hakone brings together abundant nature, diverse cultural facilities, and time-honored historic sites, offering a wealth of enjoyment alongside your ryokan stay. Here are some nearby attractions not to be missed while staying at Hakone Suimeisou.

### 1. Hakone Open-Air Museum

Japan's first outdoor art museum, this expansive open-air space displays sculptures by world-renowned masters set amid Hakone's natural surroundings. It also features indoor galleries, including a Picasso Pavilion, offering a special experience where art and nature can be enjoyed together.

### 2. Lake Ashi and Hakone Shrine

Lake Ashi, a veritable symbol of Hakone, boasts a picturesque view set against the majestic backdrop of Mount Fuji. Crossing the lake by sightseeing boat or pirate ship is an essential part of any Hakone journey. Hakone Shrine, nestled quietly on the lake's shore within a dense grove of cedar trees, is home to the iconic Peace Torii Gate rising from the water — an unmissable photo spot.

### 3. Owakudani

This attraction lets you experience Hakone's volcanic activity firsthand. In the sulfurous zone where white smoke billows, there is the fun of trying "*kuro-tamago*" (black eggs). These eggs, boiled in the hot spring water until their shells turn black, are said by legend to add seven years to your life with each one eaten. The view of Owakudani from the Hakone Ropeway is also exceptional.

### 4. Hakone Ropeway

Running from Sounzan to Togendai, this ropeway offers one of the finest vantage points to take in a panoramic view of Hakone's mountains and valleys, along with Lake Ashi and Mount Fuji. It offers beautiful scenery in every season, but is especially spectacular during the autumn foliage.

### 5. Hakone Checkpoint

A historic site offering a glimpse into the prosperity of the Edo period. This was once a checkpoint where travelers and goods passing along the road were inspected, and full-scale reconstructed buildings and exhibits provide a window into life at the time.

### 6. Gora Park

Designed in the style of a French garden, Gora Park is a place where a variety of flowers and plants bloom beautifully throughout the four seasons. It features fountains, a rose garden, and a tropical plant conservatory, and visitors can also try their hand at pottery or glass-crafting workshops within the park.

Beyond these, Hakone offers countless museums, galleries, and walking paths, allowing you to plan a variety of trips suited to your tastes. Consulting the local maps or guide brochures provided by the ryokan, or asking staff for recommendations, is also a good approach.

## Practical Tips

Here is some practical information to help you have the best possible experience at Hakone Suimeisou.

### 1. Reservations

Popular high-end ryokan like Hakone Suimeisou can be very difficult to book, especially on weekends, Japanese holidays, and during peak season. To secure your preferred dates, it is best to book at least a few months in advance, particularly if you plan to use the family bath. Reservations can be made through the official website or a trusted booking platform. It is advisable to move quickly when reserving specific room types (such as those with an in-room open-air bath) or a family bath time slot.

### 2. Check-in and Check-out

Check-in is generally around 3:00 PM, and check-out around 10:00 or 11:00 AM. As this can vary by ryokan, be sure to confirm the exact times when finalizing your reservation. If you need a late check-in or early check-out, it is best to contact the ryokan in advance to make arrangements.

### 3. Language

Ryokan staff can often communicate basic English, though not every staff member may be fluent. Knowing a few basic Japanese greetings or simple phrases can help facilitate smoother communication. Using a translation app is also a good approach.

### 4. Luggage Service

A paid service (such as Yamato Transport) is available to carry your luggage from major stations, like Hakone-Yumoto Station, directly to the ryokan. This convenient service saves you the trouble of carrying heavy bags. Once you arrive at the ryokan, they will also carry your luggage to your room.

### 5. Special Requests

If you have special requests — such as food allergies, a particular diet (vegetarian, etc.), or arranging a cake or flowers to celebrate an anniversary — it is best to inform the ryokan in advance, either at the time of booking or before your stay. The ryokan will do its best to make your special moment even more memorable.

### 6. Attire

Wearing a yukata within the ryokan is customary. Wearing the yukata provided in your room as you move about the ryokan and head to the bath is part of the ryokan culture. The yukata offers a comfortable and charming experience. For going outside, neat and comfortable clothing is recommended.

### 7. Payment

In addition to cash (Japanese yen), major credit cards (Visa, Mastercard, JCB, etc.) are accepted. However, some small shops or buses may only accept cash, so it is wise to carry a small amount of cash as well.

### 8. Wi-Fi

Most high-end ryokan offer free Wi-Fi in guest rooms and common areas. Confirm this before your stay so you can prepare in advance if needed.

### 9. Traveling with Children

If visiting with children, it is important to check the ryokan's policies regarding children, including rates and the availability of children's meals and amenities. Some ryokan may restrict stays for children under a certain age.

Familiarizing yourself with this practical information in advance will allow you to enjoy an even richer and more satisfying luxury ryokan experience at Hakone Suimeisou.

## Access

Hakone is highly accessible from Tokyo, making it a popular area for many travelers. The journey to Hakone Suimeisou is itself a beautiful part of the experience.

### 1. From Tokyo to Hakone

*   **Odakyu Romancecar**: The most convenient way, connecting directly from Tokyo's Shinjuku Station to Hakone-Yumoto Station. The journey takes about 85 minutes, offering a comfortable ride with beautiful scenery outside the window. Purchasing a "Hakone Freepass" is economical, as it provides discounts on transportation costs and admission to some tourist facilities.
*   **JR Shinkansen**: Another option is taking the JR Tokaido Shinkansen (Kodama service) from Tokyo Station to Odawara Station, then transferring to a train bound for Hakone-Yumoto Station. The Tokyo-Odawara segment takes about 30 minutes.

### 2. From Hakone-Yumoto Station to Hakone Suimeisou

Hakone-Yumoto Station serves as the gateway and transportation hub for sightseeing in the Hakone area. There are several ways to travel onward, depending on the exact location of the ryokan.

*   **Ryokan Shuttle Service**: Some luxury ryokan offer a complimentary shuttle service from Hakone-Yumoto Station to the ryokan. It is most convenient to confirm shuttle availability and schedules with the ryokan at the time of booking.
*   **Hakone Tozan Railway**: You can take the Hakone Tozan Railway from Hakone-Yumoto Station toward Gora. Depending on the ryokan's location, you would disembark at the appropriate station and then continue on foot or by taxi. This train climbs the steep mountain terrain, offering breathtaking scenery along the way.
*   **Hakone Tozan Bus**: There is a bus stop in front of Hakone-Yumoto Station. It is also a good idea to check the bus stop nearest the ryokan and take the bus, which connects to major sightseeing spots throughout Hakone.
*   **Taxi**: If you have a lot of luggage or are traveling with a group, taking a taxi from Hakone-Yumoto Station is the fastest and most convenient option.

### 3. Traveling by Car

If visiting by rental car, be sure to check in advance whether the ryokan has parking available. Hakone has a lot of mountainous terrain, so caution is needed while driving, and road conditions may be poor in winter due to snowfall.

The road to Hakone Suimeisou is more than just a journey — it is the beginning of an experience in which you get to savor Hakone's beautiful nature in advance. May your comfortable and safe journey open the curtain on an unforgettable ryokan experience.
