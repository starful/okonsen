## Introduction

Kusatsu is famous for **acidic, mineral-rich water** and the open-air *yubatake* (hot-water field) at the center of town. **Hotel Sakurai** is a large, traveler-friendly stay that still feels rooted in local onsen culture — wide bath facilities, seasonal dining, and easy access to the village streets.

## Baths & water

Kusatsu water is notably strong (high acidity / sulfur character). Hotel Sakurai draws from the town’s renowned sources and offers indoor and outdoor soaking options. Start with shorter dips if you are new to Kusatsu’s intensity, and rinse thoroughly before entering communal baths.

Confirm tattoo and private-bath (*kashikiri*) policies at booking time if that matters for your group.

## Rooms & dining

Rooms mix traditional ryokan cues with hotel-scale comfort. Dinner is typically multi-course Japanese kaiseki with Gunma and highland ingredients; breakfast is a full Japanese set. Ask about dietary needs when you reserve.

## Getting there & tips

*   **Access:** Train toward Naganohara-Kusatsuguchi, then bus into Kusatsu Onsen; confirm the last bus before evening arrivals.
*   **Town circuit:** Pair your hotel soak with an evening walk around the yubatake and, if you like, a public-bath hop.
*   **Season:** Weekends and autumn foliage fill up early — book ahead.
